\section{Model}

\subsection{Environment}

Consider a metropolitan area consisting of a finite set of locations $S = \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$. These locations are partitioned into municipalities $g \in G$:
\[
S = \bigcup_{g \in G} S^g, \qquad S^g \cap S^{g'} = \emptyset \quad \text{for } g \neq g'.
\]

Each location $i \in S$ is endowed with a fixed supply of land $\bar{H}_i$, allocated between residential and commercial use by local zoning:
\[
\bar{H}_i^R + \bar{H}_i^F = \bar{H}_i,
\]
where $\bar{H}_i^R$ is residential floor space capacity and $\bar{H}_i^F$ is commercial floor space capacity. Both are chosen by the municipal government and assumed to be fully utilized in equilibrium. Land at each location is owned by absentee landlords who collect $q_i^R\bar H_i^R + q_i^F\bar H_i^F$ and consume it outside the metropolitan area; this rental income has no further equilibrium feedback — it does not enter household budgets or municipal welfare.

\subsubsection{Transportation}
Locations are connected by a transportation network $(S, E)$, where $E \subseteq S \times S$ is a set of directed edges (links). Transportation investment $I_{k\ell}$ is chosen at the \emph{edge} level, for each $(k,\ell) \in E$ The utility cost of traveling through edge $(k,\ell)$ is
\[
d_{k\ell} = exp(\kappa \times time_{k\ell}),
\]
where $time_{k\ell}$ is the travel time on edge $(k,\ell)$ and $\kappa > 0$ is a scale parameter, following \cite{Ahlfeldt2015-zb}. The travel time $time_{k\ell}$ is a function of the edge-level traffic $n_{k\ell}$ and investment $I_{k\ell}$:
\[
time_{k\ell} = \delta_{kl}^{0}+\delta_{kl}^{1}\frac{n_{k\ell}^{\rho}}{I_{k\ell}^{\beta}}.
\]
Following \cite{Fajgelbaum2020-yv}, $\delta_{kl}^{0}$ is the free-flow travel time on edge $(k,\ell)$, $\delta_{kl}^{1} > 0$ is a scale parameter, and $\rho, \beta > 0$ are elasticities of travel time with respect to traffic and investment respectively.
Therefore for a given route r, the commuting cost $\tau_{ij}$ between residence $i$ and workplace $j$ is the product of the costs of all edges along that route:
\[
\tau_{ijr} = \prod_{(k,\ell) \in r_{ij}} d_{k\ell}(I_{k\ell}, n_{k\ell}),
\]
where $r_{ij}$ is the route chosen by commuters from $i$ to $j$.

There are three types of agents: households, competitive firms, and governments. Households choose residence and workplace locations. Firms produce a freely tradable good using labor and commercial floor space. Government decisions operate at two levels: municipal governments choose local zoning, and a metropolitan transportation authority chooses investment across all links. In the decentralized equilibrium, municipalities and the transportation authority choose policies simultaneously, each treating the other's decisions as given.

\subsection{Households}

A household chooses a residence location $i$, a workplace $j$, and a route $r$ from a finite, exogenously given choice set $R_{ij}$ of feasible routes between $i$ and $j$ (e.g. the $k$ shortest GIS paths, or distinct road/transit alternatives) to maximize utility. Preferences over the numaire good $Q_{ij}$ and residential floor space $h_{ij}^R$ are Cobb-Douglas:
\[
u_{ijr}(\omega) = \frac{B_i(N_i,L_i)}{\tau_{ijr}}
\left(\frac{Q_{ij}}{\alpha}\right)^{\!\alpha}
\left(\frac{h_{ij}^R}{1-\alpha}\right)^{\!1-\alpha}
v_{ij,r}(\omega),
\]
where $B_i(N_i,L_i) = \bar B_i/\phi(N_i,L_i) > 0$ is the effective residential amenity at location $i$: an exogenous location-specific level $\bar B_i$, discounted by the congestion disutility $\phi(N_i,L_i)$ from local residential and workplace density (defined below in \S Municipal Zoning). $\alpha \in (0,1)$ is the expenditure share on the composite good, $\tau_{ijr}$ is the commuting cost along route $r$, and $\{v_{ij,r}(\omega)\}$ is a vector of idiosyncratic shocks, jointly distributed according to a nested (generalized extreme value) Fréchet copula with cumulative distribution function
\begin{align}
    \label{GEV}
    F(\{v_{ij,r}\}) = \exp\!\left[-\sum_{ij \in S^{2}} T_iE_j\left(\sum_{r\in R_{ij}}v_{ij,r}^{-\theta}\right)^{\!\epsilon/\theta}\right], \qquad \theta \geq \epsilon > 1.
\end{align}
The outer exponent is $+\epsilon/\theta$, not $-\epsilon/\theta$: a valid CDF must satisfy $F\to1$ as any $v_{ij,r}\to\infty$ holding the rest fixed, which pins the sign down. $T_i,E_j$ enter at the outer $(i,j)$ nest only, not inside the inner route sum: they are location-pair accessibility scales, not route-specific ones, exactly as in the single-route model. Setting $R_{ij}=\{r\}$ for every $(i,j)$ collapses \eqref{GEV} to the original univariate Fréchet marginal $\Pr(v_{ij}\leq z)=\exp(-T_iE_jz^{-\epsilon})$, confirming the nesting strictly generalizes it; $\theta\geq\epsilon$ is required for \eqref{GEV} to be a valid (positively correlated within-nest) GEV copula.

The household's budget constraint is
\[
Q_{ij} + q_i^R h_{ij}^R \leq w_j,
\]
where $q_i^R$ is the residential rent at location $i$ and $w_j$ is the wage at workplace $j$ — local congestion no longer enters the budget directly; it operates entirely through the amenity $B_i(N_i,L_i)$ above. Optimal demands are $Q_{ij}^* = \alpha w_j$ and $h_{ij}^{R*} = (1-\alpha)w_j/q_i^R$, giving the deterministic component of indirect utility for a given route,
\[
\tilde{V}_{ijr} = \frac{B_i(N_i,L_i)\, w_j}{\tau_{ijr}\,(q_i^R)^{1-\alpha}}.
\]
Residential floor space clears when aggregate demand from all residents of $i$ exhausts the fixed supply $\bar H_i^R$: summing $h_{ij}^{R*}$ over workplaces $j$, weighted by the population $N\pi_{ij}$ commuting from $i$ to $j$,
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:rent-clearing}
    \sum_j N\pi_{ij}\,\frac{(1-\alpha)w_j}{q_i^R} = \bar H_i^R
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    q_i^R = \frac{(1-\alpha)\sum_j N\pi_{ij}\,w_j}{\bar H_i^R},
\end{align}
which pins down $q_i^R$ as a function of the commuting-flow pattern $\{\pi_{ij}\}$, wages, and the zoning-determined residential supply $\bar H_i^R$.

\paragraph{Upper nest choice}: applying the GEV choice-probability formula to \eqref{GEV}, households compare residence-workplace pairs using the route-choice-corrected value $\tilde V_{ij}$, with $T_i,E_j$ entering exactly as in the single-route model:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:pi}
    \pi_{ij} = \frac{T_i E_j \tilde{V}_{ij}^{\epsilon}}{\sum_{i'j' \in S^{2}} T_{i'} E_{j'} \tilde{V}_{i'j'}^{\epsilon}} = \frac{T_iE_j\tilde V_{ij}^\epsilon}{\Phi},
\end{align}
where $\tilde{V}_{ij}=\dfrac{B_i(N_i,L_i)\,w_j}{\tau_{ij}(q_{i}^R)^{1-\alpha}}$ and $\tau_{ij} = \left(\sum_{r\in R_{ij}}\tau_{ijr}^{-\theta}\right)^{-1/\theta}$ is the Fréchet mean of route costs.such that the population choosing i,j pair is:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:pi-pop}
    N_{ij} =T_iE_j\tilde V_{ij}^\epsilon \frac{N}{\Phi},
\end{align}

\paragraph{Two notions of expected utility.} Write $\Phi_i\equiv\sum_j T_iE_j\tilde V_{ij}^\epsilon$ for location $i$'s own contribution to $\Phi=\sum_{i'}\Phi_{i'}$, so that $N_i/N=\Phi_i/\Phi$ by \eqref{eq:pi-pop}. Two distinct expected-utility objects can be built from this, and downstream sections use both — it matters which is used where.

If a household is free to choose its residence from the \emph{entire} metro (not just a workplace, given a residence), a standard property of Fréchet/GEV extreme-value shocks pins down its expected utility exactly. $W_i\equiv\max_j(\ln \tilde V_{ij}+\ln v_{ij}(\omega))$ — the best a household could do conditional on living at $i$ — is itself Gumbel-distributed with location parameter $\ln\Phi_i$, since it is the max of independent Fréchet-shocked draws across $j\in S$; residence choice is therefore equivalent to a further logit over the $|S|$ "meta-alternatives" $i=1,\dots,|S|$, each with systematic value $\ln\Phi_i$. Applying McFadden's classical result for extreme-value choice models to \emph{that} reduced problem — the expected utility conditional on any single alternative being the argmax equals the same unconditional expected-max, regardless of which alternative wins — gives
\[
\mathbb E[\text{utility}\mid \text{residence freely chosen, realized as }i] \;=\; \Gamma\!\left(1-\tfrac1\epsilon\right)\Phi^{1/\epsilon} \;=\; \bar U \qquad\text{for every }i,
\]
the \emph{same} value at every location. This is not a modeling assumption; it is a mechanical consequence of the compensating-shock property of Fréchet/GEV shocks: a household that ends up at a less attractive $i$ (lower $\Phi_i$) must have drawn an unusually favorable idiosyncratic shock to get there, and in expectation this exactly offsets $i$'s lower systematic value. $\bar U$ is therefore the right welfare object for an agent representing the \emph{whole} population, treating where-everyone-lives as a free margin — this is exactly the role of the transportation authority and social planner, whose objectives (\eqref{eq:TA-problem}, \eqref{eq:SP-problem}) are $\Gamma(1-1/\epsilon)\Phi^{1/\epsilon}=\bar U$, built from the aggregate $\Phi$, correctly.

A municipal government represents a different population: \emph{incumbents}, households whose residence at $i$ is already a given, sunk fact, not a margin the municipality is optimizing over. For such a household, only the workplace margin $j$ remains open, and holding $i$ exogenously fixed rather than integrating over the residence choice gives a second, genuinely local object,
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:Ubar-i}
    \bar U_i \;\equiv\; \Gamma\!\left(1-\tfrac1\epsilon\right)\Phi_i^{1/\epsilon},
\end{align}
the expected value of $\max_j(\ln\tilde V_{ij}+\ln v_{ij}(\omega))$ taken directly, with no further reduction over $i$. Unlike $\bar U$, $\bar U_i$ varies with $i$'s own $\bar B_i,\phi_i,q_i^R$ (through $\Phi_i$) and is generically smaller than $\bar U$, since $\Phi_i$ is only one summand of $\Phi=\sum_{i'}\Phi_{i'}$.

\textbf{Revised 2026-07-27, superseding the paragraph above's original conclusion (Open Model Question \#18):} the municipal objective in \S Municipal Zoning below uses $\bar U$, not $\bar U_i$ — i.e. $\bar U_i$ is retired as a distinct object entering any agent's objective, though \eqref{eq:Ubar-i} is left stated above since it remains a well-defined quantity. The original (2026-07-23) argument for $\bar U_i$ was that a municipality representing incumbents shouldn't use $\bar U$, since $\bar U$ is fixed for a small player and gives its zoning choice no bite (the rejected-$N^g\cdot\bar U$ degeneracy, formerly a footnote in \S Municipal Zoning). That argument implicitly paired "use $\bar U$" with "also treat $\Phi$ as approximately fixed with respect to the municipality's own choice" (the small-open-location approximation) — it is \emph{that combination}, not the use of $\bar U$ per se, that produces the degeneracy. Using $\bar U$ \emph{without} the small-open-location approximation — i.e.\ differentiating the exact $\bar U=\Gamma(1-1/\epsilon)\Phi^{1/\epsilon}$ with respect to a single municipality's $\bar H_i^R$, fully accounting for how this moves $\Phi$ — gives a perfectly well-posed, non-degenerate first-order condition (\S Municipal Zoning). The payoff is that every welfare-representing agent in this model — municipality, metropolitan zoning authority, transportation authority, social planner — now maximizes the identical object $\bar U$; the only remaining differences across agents are which variables each one controls (scope: $S^g$ vs.\ $S$; jointly with $I_{k\ell}$ or not) and whether its problem is solved as a Nash best-response or as part of a simultaneous joint optimum — not which utility index it uses. This is the throughline connecting today's two revisions (population-weighting, above, and this one).

\paragraph{Lower nest choice}: conditional on choosing $(i,j)$, the household chooses a route $r \in R_{ij}$. Following \citet{Allen2022-ms}, we can rewrite $\tau_{ij} = \left(\sum_{r\in R_{ij}}\tau_{ijr}^{-\theta}\right)^{-1/\theta}$ such that
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:pi-route}
    \tau=((I-A)^{-1})^{-1/\theta},
\end{align}
where $A$ is a matrix with elements (k,l) is $d_{kl}^{-\theta}$,such that for matrix $\tau$, the element (i,j) is $\tau_{ij}$.
Accordingly, we can derive the link intensity as follows:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:edge-intensity}
    \pi_{jk}^{k\ell} = (\frac{\tau_{ij}}{\tau_{ik}d_{kl}\tau_{lj}})^{\theta}
\end{align}
Therefore I can derive the traffic on edge (k,l) as follows:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:edge-traffic}
    n_{k\ell} = \sum_{ij \in S^{2}}N_{ij}\pi_{jk}^{k\ell}
\end{align}

\subsection{Firms}

At each location $j$, competitive firms produce a freely tradable good using labor $L_j$ and commercial floor space $H_j^F$:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:firm-production}
Y_j = A_j L_j^{\gamma}(H_j^F)^{1-\gamma},
\end{align}
where $A_j > 0$ is location-specific productivity and $\gamma \in (0,1)$. Taking wages $w_j$ and commercial rents $q_j^F$ as given, profit maximization yields:
\[
L_{j}=(\frac{\gamma A_{j}}{w_{j}})^{1/(1-\gamma)}H_{j}^F, \qquad
q_{j}^F=(1-\gamma)(\frac{\gamma}{w_{j}})^{\gamma/(1-\gamma)}A_{j}^{1/(1-\gamma)}.
\]

\paragraph{Productivity agglomeration.} Local productivity is itself subject to a Marshallian externality from local employment density:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:agglomeration}
    A_j = \bar A_j\,L_j^{\zeta}, \qquad \bar A_j>0,\ \zeta\in(0,1-\gamma),
\end{align}
where $\bar A_j$ is the exogenous fundamental and $\zeta$ is the agglomeration elasticity. As with the congestion term $\phi(N_i,L_i)$ in \S Municipal Zoning, individual firms are atomistic and take $A_j$ — hence $L_j$ — as given when maximizing profit; the externality only appears once the labor-demand condition above is solved out in equilibrium. Substituting \eqref{eq:agglomeration} into $L_j=(\gamma A_j/w_j)^{1/(1-\gamma)}\bar H_j^F$ and solving explicitly for $L_j$,
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:labor-demand-agglom}
    L_j = \left[\frac{\gamma\,\bar A_j\,(\bar H_j^F)^{1-\gamma}}{w_j}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma-\zeta}}.
\end{align}
The restriction $\zeta<1-\gamma$ is required for this exponent to be finite and positive, i.e.\ for equilibrium local labor demand to remain strictly decreasing in $w_j$ — a stability condition on the strength of the externality relative to the labor share, playing the same role as the $a>1$ convexity condition already imposed on $\phi$.

\subsection{Municipal Zoning}

Land at each location competes for residential and commercial use. Each municipality $g \in G$ chooses a zoning wedge $\{t_i\}_{i \in S^g} \geq 0$ on commercial land use at each location: procedural burden — discretionary review, permitting delay, litigation risk, compliance — drives a wedge between the residential and commercial rents the same underlying land parcel could command. Because the same underlying land parcel can be put to either use, competitive landowners allocate it to whichever use pays the higher effective return; if both uses are active in equilibrium, this competition equalizes returns at the margin:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:arbitrage}
    t_{i}=\frac{q_{i}^{F}}{q_{i}^{R}}
\end{align}
In general $t_{i}>1$ implies a pro-residential zoning, wheras $t_{i}<1$ implies a pro-commercial zoning. Combined with the residential rent-clearing condition \eqref{eq:rent-clearing}, the arbitrage condition \eqref{eq:arbitrage} gives a one-to-one mapping between the zoning wedge $t_i$ and the direct-quantity instrument $\bar H_i^R$:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:arbitrage1}
    t_i\,\frac{(1-\alpha)\sum_j N\pi_{ij}w_j}{\bar H_i^R} = q_i^F=(1-\gamma)\left(\frac{\gamma}{w_{i}}\right)^{\gamma/(1-\gamma)}A_{i}^{1/(1-\gamma)}.
\end{align}
Treating $q_i^F$ as locally fixed (the same simplification used below and in \S Transportation Authority, deferring the further GE feedback through $w_i,A_i$ to the quantification stage) and using $\partial q_i^R/\partial \bar H_i^R=-q_i^R/\bar H_i^R$ from \eqref{eq:rent-clearing}, implicit differentiation of $t_i q_i^R=q_i^F$ gives
\begin{align}
    \label{zoning-foc-tax}
    \frac{d\bar H_i^R}{dt_i} = \frac{\bar H_i^R}{t_i} > 0,
\end{align}
so $\bar H_i^R(t_i)$ is strictly increasing: the map between the zoning-wedge instrument and the direct-quantity instrument is one-to-one wherever both uses are active.

The municipality sets $\{t_i\}$ to maximize the total welfare of its residents. In line with the political economy of local land use \citep{Mast2024-zi, Rollet2025-mk}, the municipal government, acting as a representative of its residents, trades off (1) lower rents and reduced worker-congestion from shrinking commercial land — both favor a higher zoning wedge — against (2) the residential crowding that comes with the resulting in-migration, and (3) the loss of local wage income to residents who both live and work at $i$, both of which favor a lower wedge.
\[
\max_{\{t_i\}_{i \in S^g}}\; W^g = \sum_{i \in S^g} \bar{U}_{i}
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\eqref{eq:arbitrage},\ \ \bar{H}_i^R + \bar{H}_i^F = \bar{H}_i,
\]
where $\bar{U}_i =\bar{U}$ — the expected utility of a household under free mobility conditions, there is a disamenitiy $\phi(N_i,L_i)$ from local residential and workplace density, which is increasing in both $N_i$ and $L_i$:
\[
\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial N_i} > 0, \qquad \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial L_i} > 0,
\]
where $N_i$ denotes residential population and $L_i$ denotes employment at location $i$ (defined above). I parametrize the disutility in Cobb-Douglas form:
\begin{align}
    \phi(N_i, L_i) = \eta\, N_i^{a}\, L_i^{b}, \qquad \eta > 0,\ a, b > 0,
\end{align}
so that $B_i(N_i,L_i) = \bar B_i\,\eta^{-1} N_i^{-a}L_i^{-b}$: an exogenous amenity level $\bar B_i$, marked down by local residential and workplace density. Since $\bar H_i^F = \bar H_i - \bar H_i^R$, the chain rule reduces the municipal first-order condition to a single comparison,
\begin{align}
    \label{zoning-foc}
\frac{dW^g}{dt_i} &= \left(\frac{\partial W^g}{\partial \bar{H}_i^R} - \frac{\partial W^g}{\partial \bar{H}_i^F}\right)\\
&\quad\times\frac{d\bar H_i^R}{dt_i} = 0,
\end{align}
so the optimum reduces to signing $\partial W^g/\partial \bar H_i^R - \partial W^g/\partial \bar H_i^F$, given $d\bar H_i^R/dt_i \neq 0$ (\eqref{zoning-foc-tax} above). The Channels paragraph below derives this comparison by implicit differentiation, treating $\partial N_i/\partial\bar H_i^R$ etc. as unspecified GE objects; the remainder of this subsection derives the same comparison rigorously, as a Lagrangian subject to the equilibrium conditions municipality $g$'s choice touches — the same "problem subject to equilibrium" treatment already used for the transportation authority (\S Transportation Authority, per Open Question \#17), rather than approximating the GE feedback away from the start.

\paragraph{The municipal problem as a Lagrangian.} $N_i,L_i,\phi_i,q_i^R,w_i$ are equilibrium objects, not primitives. Following \eqref{eq:TA-lagrangian}'s construction, and pulling $\phi_i$ out as its own variable — exactly as $d_{k\ell}$ is pulled out in \eqref{eq:TA-lagrangian}, so that $N_i,L_i$ enter nowhere except their own defining identities and $\phi_i$'s — municipality $g$'s problem is
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:muni-lagrangian}
\mathcal L^g = {}& \sum_{i\in S^g} \Gamma\!\left(1-\tfrac1\epsilon\right)\Phi^{1/\epsilon}
+ \sum_{i\in S^g}\chi_i\Bigl[\phi_i-\eta N_i^aL_i^b\Bigr]
+ \sum_{i'j'\in S^2}\lambda_{i'j'}\Bigl[T_{i'}E_{j'}\tilde V_{i'j'}^\epsilon - \pi_{i'j'}\Phi\Bigr] \nonumber\\
&+ \sum_{i\in S^g}\sigma_i\Bigl[N_i - N\textstyle\sum_j\pi_{ij}\Bigr]
+ \sum_{i\in S^g}\rho_i\Bigl[L_i - N\textstyle\sum_j\pi_{ji}\Bigr] \nonumber\\
&+ \sum_{i\in S^g}\eta_i^q\Bigl[q_i^R\bar H_i^R - (1-\alpha)\textstyle\sum_jN\pi_{ij}w_j\Bigr]
+ \sum_j\eta_j^w\Bigl[L_j(w_j) - N\textstyle\sum_i\pi_{ij}\Bigr] \nonumber\\
&+ \sum_{i\in S^g}\nu_i\Bigl[\bar H_i-\bar H_i^R-\bar H_i^F\Bigr],
\end{align}
where $\Phi_i\equiv\bar B_i^\epsilon\phi_i^{-\epsilon}\sum_jT_iE_jw_j^\epsilon\tau_{ij}^{-\epsilon}(q_i^R)^{-\epsilon(1-\alpha)}$ and $\Phi\equiv\sum_{i'\in S}\Phi_{i'}$ as before (\S Households), and $\tilde V_{ij}=\bar B_iw_j/(\phi_i\tau_{ij}(q_i^R)^{1-\alpha})$. \textbf{Revised 2026-07-27 (second revision):} the objective's leading term is now $\Gamma(1-1/\epsilon)\Phi^{1/\epsilon}=\bar U$ — the aggregate index, summed over $i\in S^g$ (each term identical, since $\bar U$ does not vary by $i$; equivalently $W^g=|S^g|\bar U$) — replacing the local $\Phi_i^{1/\epsilon}=\bar U_i$ used before today (\S Households, "Two notions of expected utility"). This is literally the same leading term as \eqref{eq:TA-lagrangian}'s and \eqref{eq:SP-problem}'s, restricted here to the choice variables $\{\bar H_i^R\}_{i\in S^g}$ only. $\lambda_{i'j'}$ (household optimality, all OD pairs — a zoning change at $i\in S^g$ moves $q_i^R$ and $\phi_i$, hence $\tilde V_{ij}$, hence potentially every $\pi_{i'j'}$ through $\Phi$), $\eta_i^q$ (rent clearing), and $\eta_j^w$ (labor clearing, $L_j(w_j)\equiv(\gamma A_j/w_j)^{1/(1-\gamma)}\bar H_j^F$ under \eqref{eq:labor-demand-agglom}) reuse the same multipliers already carrying these meanings in \eqref{eq:TA-lagrangian}; $\nu_i$ (land identity) reuses the one already used in \eqref{eq:SP-problem}. Three multipliers are new: $\chi_i$ (the pulled-out congestion identity), and $\sigma_i,\rho_i$ on the population and employment identities $N_i=N\sum_j\pi_{ij}$, $L_i=N\sum_j\pi_{ji}$ — needed, exactly as in the social planner's Lagrangian (\S Social Planner), to pin down $N_i,L_i$ for the pulled-out congestion identity $\phi_i=\eta N_i^aL_i^b$, since $\Phi_i$ (hence $\Phi$, hence $\bar U$) depends on $\phi_i$ even though neither $N_i$ nor $L_i$ enters the objective directly. No separate multiplier is needed for the arbitrage condition \eqref{eq:arbitrage}: $\bar H_i^R$ (not $t_i$) is the genuine Lagrangian choice variable, and $t_i^*$ is recovered afterward exactly as in Definition~1.

\begin{remark}[Why $\chi_i,\sigma_i,\rho_i,\eta_i^q$ are scoped to $S^g$, and what that costs]
Note precisely which sums are restricted to $S^g$ and which are not: $\chi_i,\sigma_i,\rho_i,\eta_i^q,\nu_i$ — restricted; $\lambda_{i'j'},\eta_j^w$ — unrestricted, spanning the whole metro $S$. The restriction is deliberate and correct for what municipality $g$ is actually choosing: it has no $\bar H_i^R$ to set at $i\notin S^g$, so it has no reason to carry $i\notin S^g$'s own congestion or rent-clearing identity as a constraint. But $\lambda_{i'j'}$ has to be unrestricted regardless — $\Phi$ is metro-wide, so $g$'s own zoning move reprices \emph{every} OD pair, not just its own. This asymmetry is exactly what \S Welfare Comparison's closing Remark shows is the source of the decentralized-vs-planner welfare gap: $\lambda_{ij}$ for any OD pair touching a location outside $S^g$ (as either residence or workplace) is pinned down, in \emph{$g$'s own} problem, by a first-order condition missing the $\sigma_{i'},\rho_{i'},\eta_{i'}^q$ terms that location would contribute if it were in $g$'s own scope — terms that \emph{are} present in the metropolitan authority's and planner's Lagrangians, which span all of $S$ for every identity, not just for $\lambda$ and $\eta^w$.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{$N_i,L_i$: clean first-order conditions.} Having pulled $\phi_i$ out, $N_i$ and $L_i$ appear only in the $\chi_i$ bracket and their own identity bracket — neither enters the objective directly:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:muni-NL-focs}
\frac{\partial\mathcal L^g}{\partial N_i}=0:\;\; \sigma_i = \chi_i\,\frac{a\phi_i}{N_i},
\qquad
\frac{\partial\mathcal L^g}{\partial L_i}=0:\;\; \rho_i = \chi_i\,\frac{b\phi_i}{L_i}.
\end{align}
$\bar H_i^R,\bar H_i^F$ each appear only in their own market-clearing bracket and the shared land-identity bracket — no $\lambda_{i'j'}$ appearance, since neither enters $\tilde V_{ij}$ directly — giving the clean pair
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:muni-land-foc}
\frac{\partial\mathcal L^g}{\partial \bar H_i^R}=0:\;\; \nu_i=\eta_i^q q_i^R,
\qquad
\frac{\partial\mathcal L^g}{\partial \bar H_i^F}=0:\;\; \nu_i=\eta_i^w\,\frac{\partial L_i}{\partial \bar H_i^F}\bigg|_{w_i\text{ fixed}} = \eta_i^w\,\frac{(1-\gamma)L_i}{(1-\gamma-\zeta)\bar H_i^F},
\end{align}
the last equality exact from \eqref{eq:labor-demand-agglom}. Combining, $\eta_i^qq_i^R=\eta_i^w(1-\gamma)L_i/[(1-\gamma-\zeta)\bar H_i^F]$ — the rigorous, shadow-price version of "signing $\partial W^g/\partial\bar H_i^R-\partial W^g/\partial\bar H_i^F$," replacing the informal statement in \eqref{zoning-foc}.

\paragraph{Closing the system: $\phi_i,q_i^R,w_i$.} $\chi_i,\eta_i^q,\eta_j^w$ are not yet closed: $\phi_i,q_i^R,w_i$ each enter $\tilde V_{ij}$ directly, so each appears not only in the objective and its own market-clearing bracket but inside \emph{every} $\lambda_{i'j'}$ bracket touching it — exactly the situation $\tau_{ij}$ and $d_{k\ell}$ are in for the transportation authority (\S Transportation Authority, "Closing the system"), resolved there by defining the $\lambda$-weighted market-access object $\Lambda_{k\ell}$. \textbf{Revised 2026-07-27 (second revision):} with the objective now the aggregate $\bar U=\Gamma(1-1/\epsilon)\Phi^{1/\epsilon}$ rather than the local $\bar U_i$, its $\phi_i$-derivative picks up $\Phi_i$'s share of the \emph{whole} $\Phi$: $\partial\bar U/\partial\phi_i=\Gamma(1-1/\epsilon)\tfrac1\epsilon\Phi^{1/\epsilon-1}\partial\Phi_i/\partial\phi_i=-\bar U(\Phi_i/\Phi)/\phi_i=-N_i\bar U/(N\phi_i)$, using $\Phi_i/\Phi=N_i/N$ (\S Households). Differentiating $\mathcal L^g$ in $\phi_i$ therefore gives $\chi_i=(N_i\bar U+\epsilon\Lambda_i^\phi)/\phi_i$ with $\Lambda_i^\phi\equiv\sum_j\lambda_{ij}T_iE_j\tilde V_{ij}^\epsilon$ as before — literally identical in form to the planner's own closing condition, \eqref{eq:SP-etaq} (\S Social Planner), since both differentiate the same $\bar U$. The analogous $q_i^R$- and $w_i$-conditions pick up the same $N_i/N$ factor and define $\Lambda_i^q,\Lambda_i^w$ the same way, with $\Lambda_i^w\equiv\sum_{i'\in S}\lambda_{i'i}T_{i'}E_i\tilde V_{i'i}^\epsilon$ running over the \emph{whole} metro — every location whose residents might commute to workplace $i$ — not just $S^g$. As with $\Lambda_{k\ell}$, these are not expanded symbol-by-symbol here: they solve their own linear system jointly with every $\lambda_{i'j'}$, the same size and type of object as \S Transportation Authority's, deferred to quantification.

\paragraph{The zoning first-order condition.} Combining \eqref{eq:muni-land-foc} with the closing conditions above, municipality $g$'s zoning first-order condition for $\bar H_i^R$, $i\in S^g$, is
\[
\eta_i^q q_i^R = \eta_i^w\,\frac{(1-\gamma)L_i}{(1-\gamma-\zeta)\bar H_i^F}, \qquad \eta_i^q=\frac{(1-\alpha)\bigl[N_i\bar U+\epsilon\Lambda_i^q\bigr]}{q_i^R\bar H_i^R},
\]
term for term the same equation \S Social Planner derives as \eqref{zoning-foc-planner} for the whole metro $S$. \textbf{Revised 2026-07-27 (second revision), retiring the Channels paragraph and \texttt{zoning-foc-explicit} that appeared in this location before today:} with $\bar U_i\equiv\bar U$, there is no longer a distinct, locally-derived closed-form condition for the municipality — it solves exactly \eqref{zoning-foc-planner}, restricted to $i\in S^g$, as a Nash best response (taking every other municipality's zoning and the transportation authority's investment as given). \S Scale of the Zoning Decision and \S Social Planner below develop the identical condition for the metropolitan zoning authority ($S$, Nash) and the planner ($S$, jointly with $I_{k\ell}$); the three problems now differ only in scope and in whether $\{\bar H_i^R\}$ — and, for the planner, $\{I_{k\ell}\}$ too — is chosen as a Nash best response or a simultaneous joint optimum, the subject of \S Welfare Comparison.

\begin{remark}[The retired small-open-location special case]
Before today, a single municipality's zoning condition was obtained in closed form (the "Channels" 4-term breakdown: rent, residential congestion, worker-congestion relief, local-wage loss) by using $\bar U_i$'s \emph{local} partials — exact because $\bar U_i=\Gamma(1-1/\epsilon)\Phi_i^{1/\epsilon}$ genuinely responds to the municipality's own $\bar H_i^R$ through $\Phi_i$ alone. With $\bar U_i\equiv\bar U$, that route is gone: $\bar U$'s partials run through the \emph{whole} $\Phi$, weighted by $\Phi_i/\Phi=N_i/N$ (above), not through $\Phi_i$ alone. Approximating $\Phi$ (hence $\bar U$) as fixed with respect to a single municipality's own choice — the small-open-location approximation that produced the old closed form — would now set $\partial\bar U/\partial\bar H_i^R\approx0$ identically, making the municipality's zoning choice payoff-irrelevant to first order: a genuine degeneracy, not merely a loss of closed-form interpretability, since it would remove local zoning's bite from the model entirely. This is why that approximation is retired together with $\bar U_i$, not independently of it, and why the exact treatment above — where $\partial\bar U/\partial\bar H_i^R$ is a well-defined, generically nonzero quantity — is used instead.
\end{remark}



\subsection{Transportation Authority}

The metropolitan transportation authority chooses investment $\{I_{k\ell}\}_{(k,\ell) \in E}$ to maximize aggregate household welfare, taking the full zoning vector $\{\bar{H}_i^R, \bar{H}_i^F\}$ and the route assignment $\{r_{ij}\}$ as given. Following \citet{Bordeu2026-df}'s formalization of her local government's problem (her \S2.1.4), the maximization is stated with the equilibrium conditions of the rest of the model entered directly as constraints, alongside the budget constraint, rather than solved for separately beforehand:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:TA-problem}
\max_{\{I_{k\ell}\}_{(k,\ell)\in E}}\;\; & \Gamma\!\left(1 - \tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\Phi^{1/\epsilon} = \bar U, \qquad \Phi\equiv\sum_{i'j'}T_{i'}E_{j'}\tilde V_{i'j'}^\epsilon \\
\text{subject to} \quad
\text{(i) household optimality:}\; & \{\pi_{ij}\}\; \text{given by \eqref{eq:pi}--\eqref{eq:pi-pop};} \nonumber\\
\text{(ii) commuting-cost index:}\; & \tau_{ij} \;\text{given by \eqref{eq:pi-route};} \nonumber\\
\text{(iii) edge-level commuting cost:}\; & d_{k\ell} = \exp(\kappa\,time_{k\ell}(I_{k\ell},n_{k\ell})) \;\;(\text{\S Transportation}); \nonumber\\
\text{(iv) edge traffic:}\; & n_{k\ell} = \textstyle\sum_{ij\in S^2} N\pi_{ij}\,\pi_{ij}^{k\ell} \;\;\eqref{eq:edge-traffic}; \nonumber\\
\text{(v) labor market clearing:}\; & N\textstyle\sum_i \pi_{ij} = L_j(w_j)\equiv(\gamma A_j/w_j)^{1/(1-\gamma)}\bar H_j^F \;\;(\text{\S Firms}); \nonumber\\
\text{(vi) residential rent clearing:}\; & q_i^R \;\text{given by \eqref{eq:rent-clearing}}; \nonumber\\
\text{(vii) budget:}\; & \textstyle\sum_{(k,\ell)\in E}c_{k\ell}I_{k\ell}\leq K, \quad I_{k\ell}\geq0, \nonumber
\end{align}
where $c_{k\ell}$ is the per-unit cost of investment on edge $(k,\ell)$ and $K$ is the total infrastructure budget. \textbf{Revised 2026-07-27:} the objective is stated as the average utility $\bar U$ itself rather than $N\cdot\bar U$, to put the transportation authority, the metropolitan zoning authority (\S Scale of the Zoning Decision), and the social planner (\S Social Planner) on the same footing as the (population-unweighted, \S Municipal Zoning) municipal objective; since total population $N$ is a fixed constant, not a choice variable of this problem, this changes nothing about the maximizer or any first-order condition below, only how the objective is written. Constraints (ii) and (iii) split what was previously described only in prose as "route choice": (ii) is the bilateral commuting-cost index aggregating route costs within an OD pair (\citealt{Bordeu2026-df}'s analogous constraint, her eq.~12), and (iii) is the edge-level cost technology itself (her eq.~10) — kept as separate constraints, following her construction exactly, rather than substituted directly, because $I_{k\ell}$ enters the whole system only through (iii). Constraint (v) folds \emph{firm optimality} directly into \emph{labor-market clearing}: the firm's labor demand $L_j(w_j)$ is a simple closed-form function of $w_j$ (\S Firms), not an implicit/coupled object, so it is substituted in rather than given its own multiplier — the same combination Bordeu uses for her analogous wage/labor-clearing condition. Constraints (i)--(vi) are solved jointly, not sequentially: (iii) feeds back into (ii) and hence (i), (iv) feeds back into (iii) through $n_{k\ell}$, while (v)--(vi) simultaneously pin down $w_j$ and $q_i^R$, which enter $\tilde V_{ij}$ inside $\Phi$. This system defines $\{\pi_{ij}(I), \tau_{ij}(I), d_{k\ell}(I), n_{k\ell}(I), w_j(I), q_i^R(I)\}$ as equilibrium objects rather than free parameters, and hence also $N_i(I)=N\sum_j\pi_{ij}(I)$ and $L_i(I)=N\sum_j\pi_{ji}(I)$; $\Phi$ in the objective is the resulting reduced-form value, a function of $I$ through this whole system, not only through $\tau_{ij}$ directly.

\paragraph{Direct edge elasticity.} From $d_{k\ell}=\exp(\kappa\, time_{k\ell})$ and $time_{k\ell}=\delta_{k\ell}^0+\delta_{k\ell}^1 n_{k\ell}^\rho/I_{k\ell}^\beta$ (\S Transportation),
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:d-elasticity}
    \frac{\partial \ln d_{k\ell}}{\partial I_{k\ell}} = -\frac{\kappa\beta\,\delta_{k\ell}^1\,n_{k\ell}^\rho}{I_{k\ell}^{\beta+1}}\quad(\text{holding }n_{k\ell}\text{ fixed}), \qquad
    \frac{\partial \ln d_{k\ell}}{\partial n_{k\ell}} = \frac{\kappa\rho\,\delta_{k\ell}^1\,n_{k\ell}^{\rho-1}}{I_{k\ell}^\beta}.
\end{align}

\paragraph{Route-choice bookkeeping.} Differentiating $\tau_{ij}^{-\theta}=\sum_{r\in R_{ij}}\tau_{ijr}^{-\theta}$ and using $\ln\tau_{ijr}=\sum_{(k,\ell)\in r}\ln d_{k\ell}$,
\[
\frac{\partial \ln \tau_{ij}}{\partial d_{k\ell}} = \sum_{r\in R_{ij}}\pi_{r|ij}\,\frac{\partial \ln \tau_{ijr}}{\partial d_{k\ell}} = \frac{1}{d_{k\ell}}\sum_{r\ni(k,\ell)}\pi_{r|ij} = \frac{\pi_{ij}^{k\ell}}{d_{k\ell}}\qquad(\text{holding all other }d\text{'s fixed}),
\]
where $\pi_{r|ij}=(\tau_{ij}/\tau_{ijr})^\theta$ is the conditional route-choice probability and $\pi_{ij}^{k\ell}=\sum_{r\ni(k,\ell)}\pi_{r|ij}$ is exactly the edge-intensity object of \eqref{eq:edge-intensity}: the probability that an $(i,j)$-commuter's chosen route uses edge $(k,\ell)$. This is the object that links $\tau_{ij}$ to $d_{k\ell}$ below; it does not yet involve $I_{k\ell}$ or $n_{k\ell}$ directly, since those enter only through $d_{k\ell}$ itself, constraint (iii).

\paragraph{Formalizing "subject to equilibrium": the Lagrangian.} $\tau_{ij},d_{k\ell},w_j,q_i^R,N_i,L_i$ (and, through them, $\tilde V_{ij}$) are equilibrium objects, not primitives — they respond to $I_{k\ell}$ through the constraint system above. Following \citet{Bordeu2026-df}'s treatment of her local government's problem (her \S2.1.4 and Appendix A.3, which formalizes exactly this "planner subject to equilibrium" structure by entering every equilibrium condition into the Lagrangian with its own multiplier, rather than approximating their effect away), I write the authority's problem as a single Lagrangian over constraints (i)--(vi) of \eqref{eq:TA-problem} — six multipliers, one per constraint, matching Bordeu's own six ($\lambda_{ij},\eta_{Ri},\eta_{Fj},\gamma_{ij},\epsilon_{k\ell},\phi_{k\ell}$ in her notation) rather than substituting any of them away:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:TA-lagrangian}
\mathcal{L} = {}& \Gamma\!\left(1-\tfrac1\epsilon\right)\Phi^{1/\epsilon} - \mu\Bigl(\sum_{k\ell}c_{k\ell}I_{k\ell}-K\Bigr) + \sum_{ij}\lambda_{ij}\Bigl[T_iE_j\tilde V_{ij}^\epsilon - \pi_{ij}\Phi\Bigr] \nonumber\\
&+ \sum_{ij}\gamma_{ij}\Bigl[\tau_{ij}-((I-A)^{-1})^{-1/\theta}] + \sum_{k\ell}\psi_{k\ell}\Bigl[d_{k\ell}-\exp\bigl(\kappa\,time_{k\ell}(I_{k\ell},n_{k\ell})\bigr)\Bigr] \nonumber\\
&+ \sum_{k\ell}\varphi_{k\ell}\Bigl[n_{k\ell}-\sum_{ij}N\pi_{ij}\pi_{ij}^{k\ell}\Bigr] + \sum_i\eta_i^q\Bigl[q_i^R\bar H_i^R-(1-\alpha)\sum_jN\pi_{ij}w_j\Bigr] \nonumber\\
&+ \sum_j\eta_j^w\Bigl[L_j(w_j)-N\sum_i\pi_{ij}\Bigr],
\end{align}
where $\lambda_{ij}$, $\gamma_{ij}$, $\psi_{k\ell}$, $\varphi_{k\ell}$, $\eta_i^q$, $\eta_j^w$ are the multipliers on constraints (i)--(vi) respectively. $\lambda_{ij}$ is the metro-wide marginal social value of an additional household choosing the $(i,j)$ commute pattern; $\gamma_{ij}$ is the shadow value of the commuting-cost index $\tau_{ij}$; $\psi_{k\ell}$ is the shadow value of the edge-level cost technology $d_{k\ell}$ itself; $\varphi_{k\ell}$ is the shadow value of an additional unit of traffic on edge $(k,\ell)$.

\paragraph{First-order conditions for $I_{k\ell}$ and $n_{k\ell}$.} With $d_{k\ell}$ pulled out as its own variable, $I_{k\ell}$ and $n_{k\ell}$ each appear in $\mathcal L$ \emph{only} inside their own constraint-(iii) bracket — $I_{k\ell}$ nowhere else at all, $n_{k\ell}$ also in its own constraint-(iv) bracket. This is strictly simpler than differentiating through every OD pair routed through the edge directly. Differentiating,
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:I-foc-lagrangian}
\mu c_{k\ell} &= -\psi_{k\ell}\,\frac{\partial d_{k\ell}}{\partial I_{k\ell}} = -\psi_{k\ell}\,d_{k\ell}\,\frac{\partial \ln d_{k\ell}}{\partial I_{k\ell}}, \\
    \label{eq:phi-kl}
\varphi_{k\ell} &= \psi_{k\ell}\,\frac{\partial d_{k\ell}}{\partial n_{k\ell}} = \psi_{k\ell}\,d_{k\ell}\,\frac{\partial \ln d_{k\ell}}{\partial n_{k\ell}}.
\end{align}
Dividing \eqref{eq:I-foc-lagrangian} by \eqref{eq:phi-kl} already eliminates $\psi_{k\ell}d_{k\ell}$ and gives the ratio condition below, \emph{before} $\psi_{k\ell}$ itself is pinned down — the level of $I_{k\ell}$ relative to $\varphi_{k\ell}$ does not require solving the rest of the multiplier system.

\paragraph{Closing the system: from $\lambda_{ij}$ to $\psi_{k\ell}$.} $\psi_{k\ell}$ is itself determined by $\mathcal L$'s first-order condition in $d_{k\ell}$, which appears in its own bracket and in every $\gamma_{ij}$ bracket for OD pairs routed through $(k,\ell)$ (via $\partial\ln\tau_{ij}/\partial d_{k\ell}=\pi_{ij}^{k\ell}/d_{k\ell}$, above); $\gamma_{ij}$ in turn is determined by the FOC in $\tau_{ij}$, which appears in its own bracket and in the $\lambda_{ij}$ bracket (via $\tilde V_{ij}\propto\tau_{ij}^{-1}$). Tracing this chain through — the same linear system flagged in \S Transportation Authority's earlier discussion, not expanded symbol-by-symbol here any more than Bordeu expands hers in her main text — reproduces exactly
\[
\psi_{k\ell}\,d_{k\ell} = \epsilon\sum_{(i,j):(k\ell)\in r_{ij}}\lambda_{ij}\,T_iE_j\tilde V_{ij}^\epsilon\,\pi_{ij}^{k\ell} \;\equiv\; \epsilon\,\Lambda_{k\ell},
\]
so that \eqref{eq:I-foc-lagrangian}--\eqref{eq:phi-kl} read $\mu c_{k\ell}=-\epsilon(\partial\ln d_{k\ell}/\partial I_{k\ell})\Lambda_{k\ell}$ and $\varphi_{k\ell}=\epsilon(\partial\ln d_{k\ell}/\partial n_{k\ell})\Lambda_{k\ell}$ — identical in form to a direct-substitution derivation, but now every multiplier in the chain ($\lambda_{ij}\to\gamma_{ij}\to\psi_{k\ell}\to\varphi_{k\ell}$) is a named, separately-constrained object rather than collapsed into one step.
I call $\Lambda_{k\ell}$ edge $(k,\ell)$'s \emph{market access}: the traffic-intensity-weighted sum of accessibility gains it delivers across every OD pair routed through it, each pair weighted by its own shadow value $\lambda_{ij}$ rather than treated symmetrically. All six multiplier families — $\lambda_{ij},\gamma_{ij},\psi_{k\ell},\varphi_{k\ell},\eta_i^q,\eta_j^w$ — solve their own first-order conditions jointly, a linear system coupled because a change in any $\pi_{ij}$ moves $N_i,L_i$ (hence the congestion amenity $B_i$, hence every $\tilde V_{ij'}$), every route's cost index, every edge's traffic, every location's rent, and every workplace's wage simultaneously. I do not expand this system symbol-by-symbol here — Bordeu does not either, in her main text — beyond noting it is the same size and type of object as her "$2N+E$"-plus-route/edge-cost system (her Appendix A.3).

\paragraph{The optimal-investment condition.} Dividing \eqref{eq:I-foc-lagrangian} by \eqref{eq:phi-kl} eliminates $\Lambda_{k\ell}$ and gives, using the model's actual $d_{k\ell}$,
\begin{align}
    \label{transit-foc}
\mu c_{k\ell} = -\varphi_{k\ell}\,\frac{\partial d_{k\ell}/\partial I_{k\ell}}{\partial d_{k\ell}/\partial n_{k\ell}} = \varphi_{k\ell}\,\frac{\beta}{\rho}\,\frac{n_{k\ell}}{I_{k\ell}},
\end{align}
structurally identical to \citet{Bordeu2026-df}'s eq.~(18). Substituting \eqref{eq:phi-kl} into \eqref{transit-foc} and solving for $I_{k\ell}$,
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:I-given-mu}
I_{k\ell} = \left(\frac{\epsilon\kappa\beta}{\mu}\right)^{\!1/(\beta+1)} W_{k\ell}^{\,1/(\beta+1)}, \qquad W_{k\ell}\equiv\frac{\delta_{k\ell}^1\,n_{k\ell}^\rho\,\Lambda_{k\ell}}{c_{k\ell}}.
\end{align}
Substituting into the budget constraint $\sum_{k'\ell'}c_{k'\ell'}I_{k'\ell'}=K$ (binding at the optimum, since the marginal benefit in \eqref{eq:I-foc-lagrangian} is unbounded as $I_{k\ell}\to0^+$ whenever $W_{k\ell}>0$) pins down $(\epsilon\kappa\beta/\mu)^{1/(\beta+1)}=K/\sum_{k'\ell'}c_{k'\ell'}W_{k'\ell'}^{1/(\beta+1)}$, eliminating $\mu$ and giving the closed-form optimal investment rule
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:I-optimal}
I_{k\ell}^{*} = K\cdot\frac{W_{k\ell}^{\,1/(\beta+1)}}{\displaystyle\sum_{(k'\ell')\in E}c_{k'\ell'}\,W_{k'\ell'}^{\,1/(\beta+1)}}, \qquad W_{k\ell}\equiv\frac{\delta_{k\ell}^1\,n_{k\ell}^\rho\,\Lambda_{k\ell}}{c_{k\ell}}.
\end{align}
This is a budget-share rule: edge $(k,\ell)$'s share of the infrastructure budget rises with its own $\lambda$-weighted market access $\Lambda_{k\ell}$ and its equilibrium traffic $n_{k\ell}$ (through $\rho$: busier edges get more investment, a "fix the bottleneck" force), and falls with its own cost $c_{k\ell}$. It automatically handles the corner case: if $W_{k\ell}=0$ (no OD pair routed through $(k,\ell)$, or zero equilibrium traffic under $\rho>0$), then $I_{k\ell}^*=0$, no separate complementary-slackness case needed.


% \paragraph{A useful corollary} is the pairwise ratio, independent of both $K$ and $\mu$:
% \[
% \frac{I_{k\ell}^*}{I_{k'\ell'}^*} = \left(\frac{W_{k\ell}}{W_{k'\ell'}}\right)^{1/(\beta+1)},
% \]
% so relative investment across any two edges is determined purely by their relative congestion-technology, traffic, market-access, and cost fundamentals, regardless of the total budget. Because $\Lambda_{k\ell}$ is larger for edges serving denser, higher-value bundles of OD pairs, and restrictive zoning is exactly what suppresses the density that generates a large $\Lambda_{k\ell}$ for corridor-type edges (\S Municipal Zoning), \eqref{eq:I-optimal} is the precise channel through which fragmented zoning tilts the investment ratio away from any edge whose value depends on concentrated density — without yet distinguishing road and transit technologies structurally, which remains Open Question \#1.

% \emph{Resolution algorithm.} Following \citet{Bordeu2026-df}'s Appendix A.5 computational procedure directly: (1) given the current equilibrium $\{\pi_{ij},n_{k\ell},w_j,q_i^R\}$, solve the linear system in $(\lambda_{ij},\varphi_{k\ell},\eta_i^q,\eta_j^w)$; (2) given the equilibrium and the multipliers, compute $I_{k\ell}^*$ from \eqref{eq:I-optimal}; (3) given the new $\{I_{k\ell}^*\}$, resolve the nonlinear household/firm/market-clearing system (\S Households, \S Firms) for a new equilibrium; iterate to convergence. This is deferred to the quantification stage, as with the analogous zoning fixed point (\S Municipal Zoning).

\subsection{Equilibrium}

\begin{definition}[Decentralized Equilibrium]
A decentralized equilibrium is a profile
\[
\Bigl(
\{I_{k\ell}^*\}_{(k,\ell) \in E},\;\;
\{t_i^{*}\}_{i \in S},\;\;
\{N\pi_{ij}^*\}_{i,j \in S},\;\;
\{n_{k\ell}^*\}_{(k,\ell) \in E},\;\;
\{q_i^{R*},\,q_i^{F*},\,w_i^*\}_{i \in S}
\Bigr)
\]
such that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Household optimality.} Commuting flows $\{N\pi_{ij}^*\}$ are given by \eqref{eq:pi}--\eqref{eq:pi-pop} at prices $(q_i^{R*}, w_i^*)$ and investment $I^*$, with route choice by \eqref{eq:pi-route} and route-based commuting costs $\{\tau_{ij}^*\}$.
    \item \textbf{Edge traffic.} $\{n_{k\ell}^*\}$ satisfies \eqref{eq:edge-traffic} given $\{\pi_{ij}^*\}$ and route assignment $\{r_{ij}\}$, jointly with (1) since $n_{k\ell}^*$ feeds back into $d_{k\ell}(I_{k\ell}^*,n_{k\ell}^*)$ and hence $\tau_{ij}^*$.
    \item \textbf{Firm optimality.} Wages $w_i^*$ and commercial rents $q_i^{F*}$ satisfy the firm's first-order conditions at $H_i^F = \bar{H}_i^{F*}$, with labor demand $L_i^*$ solving \eqref{eq:labor-demand-agglom} under the productivity externality \eqref{eq:agglomeration}.
    \item \textbf{Market clearing.} Residential floor space clears at $q_i^{R*}$ given by \eqref{eq:rent-clearing}, commercial floor space is fixed at $H_i^F = \bar{H}_i^{F*}$, and the labor market clears at $L_i^* = N\sum_j \pi_{ji}^*$.
    \item \textbf{Municipal Nash equilibrium.} Each municipality $g$ sets the zoning wedge $\{t_i^{*}\}_{i \in S^g}$ — equivalently, induces $\{\bar{H}_i^{R*}, \bar{H}_i^{F*}\}_{i \in S^g}$ via \eqref{zoning-foc-tax} — to satisfy the first-order condition \eqref{zoning-foc}, given $\{\bar{H}_{i'}^{R*}, \bar{H}_{i'}^{F*}\}_{i' \notin S^g}$ and $I^*$; no municipality has a profitable deviation.
    \item \textbf{Transportation authority optimality.} $\{I_{k\ell}^*\}$ solves \eqref{eq:TA-problem}, given $\{\bar{H}_i^{R*}, \bar{H}_i^{F*}\}$: equivalently, $I_{k\ell}^*$ satisfies \eqref{transit-foc} for every edge $(k,\ell)\in E$, where the multipliers $(\lambda_{ij}^*,\gamma_{ij}^*,\psi_{k\ell}^*,\varphi_{k\ell}^*,\eta_i^{q*},\eta_j^{w*})$ solve their own first-order conditions from \eqref{eq:TA-lagrangian} jointly with (1)--(4) at the equilibrium profile.
\end{enumerate}
Conditions (v) and (vi) jointly constitute the simultaneous Nash equilibrium between municipalities and the transportation authority: each set of agents takes the other's choices as given and best-responds. The equilibrium zoning wedge $t_i^{*} \equiv q_i^{F*}/q_i^{R*}$ is computed from the equilibrium quantities in this profile, not solved for as a separate object.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Scale of the Zoning Decision}

Widening the zoning decision from a single municipality to the whole metro changes the \emph{scope} of locations one zoning-setter controls ($S^g$ versus $S$). \textbf{Revised 2026-07-27 (second revision):} an earlier version of this subsection additionally framed the change as widening \emph{whose} welfare the setter maximizes — population-wide $\bar U$ for a genuinely metropolitan authority, versus the incumbent-style $\bar U_i$ then used by a single municipality (\S Households, "Two notions of expected utility," Open Model Question \#18). That second axis is now gone: \S Municipal Zoning's objective is $\bar U$ too, since $\bar U_i\equiv\bar U$ (\#18, superseded) — so scope is the \emph{only} thing this subsection's benchmark changes relative to a single municipality, and the resulting zoning condition is, unsurprisingly, exactly the social planner's.

\paragraph{The Metropolitan Zoning Authority.} Consider replacing the $|G|$ municipalities with a single zoning authority that chooses $\{t_i\}_{i \in S}$ — the zoning wedge at \emph{every} location — to maximize the same aggregate welfare as the transportation authority and social planner,
\[
\max_{\{t_i\}_{i\in S}}\; \Gamma\!\left(1-\tfrac1\epsilon\right)\Phi^{1/\epsilon}
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\eqref{eq:arbitrage},\ \ \bar H_i^R+\bar H_i^F=\bar H_i \ \ \forall i\in S,
\]
still taking the transportation authority's investment $\{I_{k\ell}\}$ as given,with one zoning agent replacing $|G|$.

\paragraph{FOC.} The metropolitan authority's Lagrangian is \S Social Planner's — $\chi_i,\sigma_i,\rho_i,\lambda_{i'j'},\eta_i^q,\eta_j^w,\nu_i$ over the aggregate objective, extending \eqref{eq:TA-lagrangian} with the land identity exactly as \eqref{eq:SP-problem} does — \emph{minus} the edge/route constraints (ii)–(iv) and the budget multiplier $\mu$, since the authority does not choose $I_{k\ell}$; it takes the transportation authority's current investment as given. But $\bar H_i^R$ never touched those constraints in the social planner's version either — it enters the Lagrangian only through the rent-clearing bracket ($\eta_i^q$) and the land-identity bracket ($\nu_i$), exactly as in \S Municipal Zoning and \S Social Planner. Removing the pieces the metropolitan authority doesn't control changes nothing about the $\bar H_i^R$-first-order condition, so the authority's zoning condition is exactly \eqref{zoning-foc-planner}.

\paragraph{Result.} With the objective and scope now matching the social planner's, \S Social Planner's "why investment does not add a term" argument extends directly: it only used that $I(t)$ is the transportation authority's own optimal response to whatever zoning is currently in place, never that zoning and investment were chosen jointly. So it applies equally whether the aggregate-objective zoning-setter is in Nash with the transportation authority (this subsection) or jointly optimizing investment with it (\S Social Planner) — \textbf{agent identity is welfare-irrelevant for the zoning margin}, given that the objective and scope match. This reinstates the original (2026-07-21) conclusion, now because the objectives were verified to match rather than assumed to. \textbf{Revised 2026-07-27 (second revision):} since \S Municipal Zoning's objective is now $\bar U$ as well, \emph{every} zoning-setter in this model — a single municipality, this metropolitan authority, the social planner — solves the identical first-order condition \eqref{zoning-foc-planner}. What remains a genuine, economically meaningful comparison is purely scope ($S^g$ versus $S$), together with whether that shared condition is solved as a Nash best response (municipality, metropolitan authority) or as part of the planner's simultaneous joint optimum — exactly what \S Welfare Comparison evaluates.

\subsection{Social Planner}

The decentralized equilibrium splits control of the metro's two instruments across two kinds of agents, each at its own scale: zoning is chosen municipality-by-municipality, each maximizing only over its own $S^g$; investment is chosen by a metropolitan authority, but taking zoning as given. The natural benchmark isolates both distortions at once — a single metropolitan government choosing \emph{both} instruments, for the \emph{whole} metro, jointly. Following \citet{Bordeu2026-df}'s comparison of her decentralized and "Centralized Planner (Metropolitan)" cases, and using exactly the Lagrangian apparatus of \S Transportation Authority, the planner's problem is
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:SP-problem}
\max_{\{t_i\}_{i\in S},\,\{I_{k\ell}\}_{(k,\ell)\in E}}\;\; & \Gamma\!\left(1 - \tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\Phi^{1/\epsilon} \\
\text{subject to} \quad & \text{(i)--(vii) of \eqref{eq:TA-problem}, and} \nonumber\\
\text{(viii) land identity:}\; & \bar H_i^R + \bar H_i^F = \bar H_i \quad \forall i\in S. \nonumber
\end{align}
The only difference from \eqref{eq:TA-problem} is that $\{\bar H_i^R,\bar H_i^F\}_{i\in S}$ — equivalently $\{t_i\}_{i\in S}$, via \eqref{eq:arbitrage} — are now genuine choice variables for every location, not parameters the constraint system takes as given; correspondingly, \eqref{eq:TA-lagrangian} gains one term, $\sum_i\nu_i[\bar H_i-\bar H_i^R-\bar H_i^F]$, with multiplier $\nu_i$ on the land identity at $i$.

\paragraph{Investment.} $I_{k\ell}$ enters the planner's Lagrangian through exactly the same terms as in \eqref{eq:TA-lagrangian} — the objective and constraints (i)–(vii) are unchanged — so the FOC is unchanged in form:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:I-optimal-SP}
I_{k\ell}^{SP} = K\cdot\frac{W_{k\ell}^{\,1/(\beta+1)}}{\displaystyle\sum_{(k'\ell')\in E}c_{k'\ell'}\,W_{k'\ell'}^{\,1/(\beta+1)}}, \qquad W_{k\ell}\equiv\frac{\delta_{k\ell}^1\,n_{k\ell}^\rho\,\Lambda_{k\ell}}{c_{k\ell}},
\end{align}
identical to \eqref{eq:I-optimal}. The planner's investment rule is the \emph{same formula}, evaluated at the jointly optimal zoning vector $\{\bar H_i^{R,SP}\}$ instead of the Nash-equilibrium one $\{\bar H_i^{R,DE}\}$ — the entire wedge between $I_{k\ell}^{SP}$ and $I_{k\ell}^{DE}$ operates through how zoning shifts $n_{k\ell}$ and $\Lambda_{k\ell}$, not through any change in the investment rule itself.

\paragraph{Zoning.} $\bar H_i^R$ enters the planner's Lagrangian only through rent clearing and the land identity, exactly as in \S Municipal Zoning — so \eqref{eq:TA-lagrangian}, extended with $\nu_i$, needs the identical $\chi_i,\sigma_i,\rho_i$ additions to correctly track $N_i,L_i,\phi_i$ (\eqref{eq:muni-lagrangian}'s construction, now over the whole $S$). \textbf{Revised 2026-07-27 (second revision):} \S Municipal Zoning's objective is $\bar U$ too (Open Model Question \#18, superseded), so there is no longer any difference to itemize here — the planner's closing conditions are, term for term, \S Municipal Zoning's own, now applied over all of $S$ rather than a single $S^g$:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:SP-etaq}
\eta_i^q = \frac{(1-\alpha)\bigl[N_i\bar U+\epsilon\Lambda_i^q\bigr]}{q_i^R\bar H_i^R}, \qquad \Lambda_i^q\equiv\sum_j\lambda_{ij}T_iE_j\tilde V_{ij}^\epsilon.
\end{align}
Combined with the analogous $\eta_i^w$ (closing through $\Lambda_i^w$), the planner's zoning condition is
\begin{align}
    \label{zoning-foc-planner}
\frac{(1-\alpha)\bigl[N_i\bar U+\epsilon\Lambda_i^q\bigr]}{\bar H_i^R} = \eta_i^w\,\frac{(1-\gamma)L_i}{(1-\gamma-\zeta)\bar H_i^F}.
\end{align}
This is literally the equation \S Municipal Zoning derives (its "The zoning first-order condition" paragraph) for a single municipality restricted to $i\in S^g$, and \S Scale of the Zoning Decision derives for the metropolitan authority restricted to Nash with the transportation authority — all three share \eqref{zoning-foc-planner}; only scope and the Nash-vs-joint solution concept differ (\S Welfare Comparison).

An earlier version of this result (2026-07-21, restated 2026-07-23) claimed \eqref{zoning-foc-planner} reduces to a distinct municipal condition (then called \texttt{zoning-foc-explicit}) plus one clean additive "cross-jurisdiction spillover" term, matching a metropolitan zoning authority that was, at the time, given the municipal-style objective $\sum_{i\in S}N_i\bar U_i$ — conflating two different objectives, since the planner here uses the population-wide $\bar U$, not the incumbent-style $\bar U_i$ (\S Households, "Two notions of expected utility"). With the metropolitan zoning authority's objective corrected to match the planner's (\S Scale of the Zoning Decision, first 2026-07-27 revision), the two conditions were shown to coincide exactly, restoring \textbf{agent identity is welfare-irrelevant for the zoning margin}. \textbf{Second 2026-07-27 revision:} with $\bar U_i\equiv\bar U$ (\#18, superseded), \S Municipal Zoning's condition is no longer even a distinct object to compare against — it \emph{is} \eqref{zoning-foc-planner}, restricted to $S^g$ — so this conclusion now holds in a stronger, more direct sense: not merely that a comparison shows agent identity irrelevant, but that there is only one zoning condition in the whole model, applied at varying scope and solved under varying (Nash or joint) solution concepts.

\paragraph{Why investment does not add a term.} $\bar H_i^R$ and $I_{k\ell}$ are chosen \emph{jointly} at the planner's optimum, each with its own first-order condition ($\partial\mathcal L^{SP}/\partial\bar H_i^R=0$, giving \eqref{zoning-foc-planner}, and, from \eqref{eq:I-foc-lagrangian}, $\partial\mathcal L^{SP}/\partial I_{k\ell}=0$ for every edge). Because $\partial\mathcal L^{SP}/\partial I_{k\ell}=0$ holds at the optimum for every $(k,\ell)$, any chain-rule term of the form $(\partial\mathcal L^{SP}/\partial I_{k\ell})(\partial I_{k\ell}^{SP}/\partial\bar H_i^R)$ is identically zero — there is no separate "feedback through $I$" term to add to \eqref{zoning-foc-planner}, regardless of what the rest of that condition looks like. The same conclusion follows without reference to the Lagrangian: define $\tilde W(t)\equiv W(t,I^{SP}(t))$; by the envelope theorem, $d\tilde W/dt_i=\partial W/\partial t_i+\sum_{k\ell}(\partial W/\partial I_{k\ell})(dI_{k\ell}^{SP}/dt_i)$, and $\partial W/\partial I_{k\ell}=\mu c_{k\ell}$ at the optimum (\eqref{eq:I-foc-lagrangian}) while $\sum_{k\ell}c_{k\ell}I_{k\ell}^{SP}(t)=K$ binds for every $t$ (fixed $K$), so $\sum_{k\ell}c_{k\ell}\,dI_{k\ell}^{SP}/dt_i=dK/dt_i=0$ and the second sum vanishes. Both checks confirm \eqref{zoning-foc-planner} needs no investment-feedback term — the correction needed relative to the earlier (2026-07-21/23) version is entirely in the zoning condition's own leading terms (missing channel (1), $\bar U$ in place of $\bar U_i$, live $\Lambda_i$'s), not in whether investment contributes an extra piece. Neither argument actually used that $I_{k\ell}$ was chosen \emph{jointly} with $\bar H_i^R$ — only that $I(t)$ is the transportation authority's own optimal response to whatever zoning is current — so both extend unchanged to a zoning-setter in Nash with the transportation authority rather than choosing $I$ itself, which is exactly \S Scale of the Zoning Decision's Metropolitan Zoning Authority.

\subsection{Welfare Comparison}

This subsection states and proves the welfare gap between the decentralized equilibrium (DE) and the social planner (SP), in two parts: a global ranking that requires no derivatives, and a first-order characterization of what drives it.

\begin{proposition}[Planner dominance]
\label{prop:SP-dominance}
Let $W^{DE}\equiv \Gamma(1-1/\epsilon)(\Phi^{DE})^{1/\epsilon}$ and $W^{SP}\equiv \Gamma(1-1/\epsilon)(\Phi^{SP})^{1/\epsilon}$ denote average household welfare (\textbf{revised 2026-07-27}: no $N$ prefix, matching \S Social Planner's objective) at the decentralized equilibrium profile $(t^{DE},I^{DE})$ (Definition~1) and at the social planner's solution $(t^{SP},I^{SP})$ of \eqref{eq:SP-problem}, respectively. Since $N$ is a fixed constant, the ranking below is unaffected by whether $W$ is stated per-capita (as here) or scaled by $N$. Then
\[
W^{SP} \;\geq\; W^{DE}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The decentralized profile $(t^{DE},I^{DE})$ satisfies constraints (i)--(vi) of \eqref{eq:TA-problem} (Definition~1, conditions 1--3) and the budget constraint (vii), and trivially satisfies the land identity (viii) since $\bar H_i^{R}+\bar H_i^F=\bar H_i$ holds by construction for any zoning profile. Hence $(t^{DE},I^{DE})$ is a feasible point of the planner's problem \eqref{eq:SP-problem} — one which the planner is free to choose but need not. Since $(t^{SP},I^{SP})$ is by definition the maximizer of the same objective $\Gamma(1-1/\epsilon)\Phi^{1/\epsilon}$ over the same feasible set, $W^{SP}\geq W^{DE}$ follows immediately. \hfill$\square$
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The investment margin is welfare-neutral at the margin]
\label{prop:SP-gap}
To leading order in a neighborhood of the decentralized equilibrium,
\[
\Delta W \equiv W^{SP}-W^{DE} \;\approx\; \sum_{i\in S} \frac{\partial W}{\partial \bar H_i^R}\bigg|_{DE}\bigl(\bar H_i^{R,SP}-\bar H_i^{R,DE}\bigr),
\]
i.e.\ the first-order welfare gap comes entirely from the zoning margin; the investment margin contributes nothing at first order.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\Delta W \approx \sum_i \dfrac{\partial W}{\partial \bar H_i^R}\bigg|_{DE}\bigl(\bar H_i^{R,SP}-\bar H_i^{R,DE}\bigr) + \sum_{k\ell}\dfrac{\partial W}{\partial I_{k\ell}}\bigg|_{DE}\bigl(I_{k\ell}^{SP}-I_{k\ell}^{DE}\bigr)$, the first-order Taylor expansion of $W$ around the DE profile in its two instrument blocks. $I^{DE}$ is the transportation authority's own optimum given $t^{DE}$ (Definition~1, condition 6), so $I^{DE}$ satisfies the same first-order condition \eqref{eq:I-foc-lagrangian} used in \S Social Planner's "Why investment does not add a term." By the identical envelope/binding-budget argument — $\partial W/\partial I_{k\ell}=\mu c_{k\ell}$ at $I^{DE}$, and $\sum_{k\ell}c_{k\ell}(I_{k\ell}^{SP}-I_{k\ell}^{DE})=K-K=0$ since both profiles exhaust the same fixed budget $K$ — the second sum is exactly $\mu\sum_{k\ell}c_{k\ell}(I_{k\ell}^{SP}-I_{k\ell}^{DE})=0$, leaving only the zoning term. \hfill$\square$
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
An earlier version of this result (2026-07-21/23) went further, claiming the remaining zoning term itself collapses to a single named "cross-jurisdiction spillover" object, using the municipal first-order condition \eqref{zoning-foc} to zero out the rest — invalid at the time, because \eqref{zoning-foc} was the first-order condition for a genuinely different object, $W^g=\sum_{i\in S^g}\bar U_i$ (municipal, incumbent-style), not the planner's own $\Gamma(1-1/\epsilon)\Phi^{1/\epsilon}$ that $\partial W/\partial\bar H_i^R$ is built from.

\textbf{Revised 2026-07-27 (second revision), replacing the argument above:} with $\bar U_i\equiv\bar U$ (Open Model Question \#18, superseded), that objection no longer applies — \eqref{zoning-foc} \emph{is} now \eqref{zoning-foc-planner} restricted to $i\in S^g$ (\S Municipal Zoning), the same equation, evaluated with the same $\Gamma(1-1/\epsilon)\Phi^{1/\epsilon}$. Yet $\partial W/\partial\bar H_i^R|_{DE}$ still does not vanish. \textbf{Third revision, same day, correcting the mechanism proposed in the second revision:} the second revision attributed this to municipalities playing a "Nash best response" versus the planner "jointly optimizing" a shared objective — this is not by itself a valid mechanism, and is worth naming as a trap: when every player in a game maximizes the identical scalar objective, a fixed point of "each player's own first-order condition holds, taking the others' \emph{current} choices as given" is mathematically the same system of equations as "every first-order condition holds simultaneously," i.e.\ a team-game Nash equilibrium coincides with the joint optimum — so "Nash vs.\ joint," stated that way, predicts \emph{no} gap at all under a shared objective, not a gap.

The actual mechanism is not strategic — it is that municipality $g$'s Lagrangian is not, in fact, the same system of equations as the planner's restricted to $i\in S^g$; it is missing equations. Look at \eqref{eq:muni-lagrangian}: $\chi_i,\sigma_i,\rho_i,\eta_i^q$ (congestion, population, employment, rent-clearing identities) are summed over $i\in S^g$ only, while $\lambda_{i'j'}$ (household optimality) and $\eta_j^w$ (labor clearing) are summed over the \emph{whole} metro $S$ — they have to be, since $g$'s own zoning move reprices every OD pair through the shared $\Phi$, regardless of where that pair lives or works. This asymmetry means: for any OD pair $(i,j)$ touching a location outside $S^g$ — whether as the residence or the workplace — $\lambda_{ij}$'s own first-order condition (which references $\sigma_i,\rho_j,\eta_i^q$, \S Households/\S Transportation Authority's construction) is missing whichever of those terms belongs to the location outside $S^g$, because that location's population, employment, congestion, or rent-clearing identity was never written into $g$'s problem in the first place — not because $g$ assumed it fixed, but because $g$ has no reason to track it. The planner's Lagrangian, and the metropolitan zoning authority's (\S Scale of the Zoning Decision, whose $\chi_i,\sigma_i,\rho_i,\eta_i^q$ span all of $S$, not a single $S^g$), do not have this gap — every location's identities are present regardless of which agent's $\bar H_i^R$ is being differentiated. So $\Lambda_i^q,\Lambda_i^w$ (\S Municipal Zoning, "Closing the system") are built from a systematically incomplete linear system for a single municipality — contaminating even the valuation of $g$'s \emph{own} locations' access to outside jobs, not only the welfare of outside locations — while being exact for the metropolitan authority and planner. This is the model's version of the classic zoning/NIMBY spillover: a municipality that restricts residential land pushes displaced residents to relocate elsewhere in the metro, raising congestion and rents wherever they land, and it neither bears nor computes that cost, because the location that absorbs it was never an equation in its own problem — while a region-wide authority, whose Lagrangian spans every location for every identity, prices it correctly.

Two further points survive from the second revision, unaffected by this correction to the mechanism itself: I do not attempt to close this into an explicit magnitude here (a closed form for how a municipality's incomplete $\Lambda_i^q,\Lambda_i^w$ system differs numerically from the planner's complete one) — for the same reason the $\Lambda$-system itself is left unexpanded symbol-by-symbol throughout this document, deferred to quantification. And Proposition~\ref{prop:SP-dominance} — the exact, global $W^{SP}\geq W^{DE}$ — does not depend on any of this and stands on its own regardless.
\end{remark}
